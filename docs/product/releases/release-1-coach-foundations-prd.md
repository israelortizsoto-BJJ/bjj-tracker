# Release 1 — Coach Foundations

## Product Requirements Document

**Product:** MatMind  
**Release:** Release 1 — Coach Foundations  
**Audience:** Founder, Product Manager, Engineering Lead, UX Designer  
**Related vision:** [Coach Experience Vision](../coach-experience-vision.md)  
**Related roadmap:** [Coach Workspace Roadmap](../coach-workspace-roadmap.md)

This PRD defines the first complete coaching experience delivered by MatMind. It bridges the Coach Workspace Roadmap into future Engineering Epics. It describes what changes for coaches after Release 1 — not how the product is built.

---

# Executive Summary

Release 1 establishes the foundation of the Coach Workspace.

Today, coaching insight in MatMind can be recorded, but it does not yet behave like coaching. Meaning lives in isolated places. Direction does not clearly connect to weekly work. Pressure observations do not reliably become continuity. The coach leaves with records, not with a clearer athlete.

Release 1 transforms those isolated coaching forms into one guided athlete development workflow.

That workflow mirrors how great coaches already think:

```text
Assess
↓
Define Future State
↓
Decide What Matters Next
↓
Train with Weekly Focus
↓
Observe Behavior Under Pressure
↓
Carry Forward
```

The purpose of Release 1 is not to collect more data. It is to make athlete development intentional, continuous, and coachable inside MatMind.

After Release 1, a coach should leave the workspace feeling:

> I understand this athlete more clearly.

Not:

> I updated another record.

This release moves MatMind from digital coaching notes toward structured athlete development — the product maturity required before voice, presence, intelligence, and family experience can compound.

# Problem Statement

Coaches already know how athlete development works. The friction is that the product does not yet organize itself around that reality.

Today's experience creates several forms of drag.

### Disconnected sections

Coaching surfaces exist as separate places rather than as one developmental arc. Assessment does not naturally lead into destination. Destination does not naturally lead into weekly reinforcement. Pressure observation does not naturally close into carry-forward. The coach must hold the connective tissue in their head.

### Isolated notes

Insight is captured, but it often remains local to the moment it was written. A strong observation from one cycle does not reliably become standing guidance for the next. Knowledge accumulates as fragments rather than as athlete understanding.

### Scattered coaching knowledge

What the coach knows about an athlete is spread across assessments, notes, weekly intentions, and competition reflections. Reconstructing a coherent picture requires memory, effort, and repeated context switching.

### No clear development flow

There is no obvious path through Current State → Future State → What Matters Next → Weekly Focus → Behavior Under Pressure → Carry Forward. Without that path, the workspace invites data entry more than developmental thinking.

### Why this creates friction

Every interruption costs coaching presence. When the product forces coaches to reconstruct the athlete, reorient between disconnected sections, or wonder what comes next, attention leaves the athlete and goes into the tool.

That friction has consequences:

- Coaching becomes administrative instead of interpretive.
- Direction weakens between sessions.
- Weekly work loses connection to long-term growth.
- Pressure truth evaporates after the moment passes.
- Continuity depends on the coach remembering what mattered last time.

Release 1 exists to remove that friction by making the coaching loop the product experience.

# Product Goals

Release 1 succeeds when the Coach Workspace changes how coaches move through athlete development.

### Reduce cognitive load

The coach should not need to remember the developmental sequence. The product should hold structure so the coach can hold judgment.

### Guide coaches naturally

Each step should feel like the next coaching decision, not the next form to complete.

### Organize coaching around athlete development

Surfaces exist to deepen understanding of the athlete — not to accumulate records for their own sake.

### Make every interaction feel intentional

A coach should always know why they are capturing something and how it connects to growth.

### Establish continuity across cycles

Insight created in one cycle should remain available as standing guidance, weekly reinforcement, or carry-forward meaning in the next.

### Establish the foundation for future Coaching Intelligence

Release 1 creates the authored developmental memory that later releases will amplify. Without coherent foundations, voice accelerates noise, presence has nothing durable to preserve, and intelligence has nothing trustworthy to compound.

# User Persona

## Primary: Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu Coach

The primary user of Release 1 is a Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu coach responsible for developing athletes over weeks, seasons, and years.

### Goals

- Understand each athlete clearly enough to coach with confidence.
- Turn long-term developmental direction into weekly reinforcement.
- Observe whether coaching is transferring under pressure.
- Preserve continuity so growth compounds instead of resetting every week.
- Spend more time thinking about the athlete and less time managing information.

### Responsibilities

- Assess where the athlete is developmentally right now.
- Define where the athlete should go next.
- Decide what deserves standing attention until intentionally changed.
- Translate direction into this week's focus.
- Interpret behavior when stakes rise.
- Carry forward what must not be lost after a session, week, or competition.

### Pain points

- Knowing more about the athlete than the system currently helps them hold.
- Losing developmental thread between weeks.
- Re-creating context every time they return to an athlete.
- Capturing notes that do not change how the next cycle is coached.
- Feeling like software work is competing with coaching work.

### Current workflow

The coach receives an athlete, forms an opinion from memory and recent observation, coaches toward something important this week, notices pressure behavior in training or competition, and hopes the right insights survive into the next cycle. Continuity depends heavily on personal memory and informal habits.

### Desired workflow

The coach opens an athlete and immediately enters a developmental conversation with the product:

1. See the athlete as they are now.
2. Confirm or refine where they are going.
3. Lock standing guidance that should persist.
4. Set this week's reinforcement.
5. Interpret what showed up under pressure.
6. Carry forward what matters next.

In that workflow, MatMind feels like an athlete understanding system — not a stack of forms.

# User Journey

Release 1 walks the coach through one continuous developmental experience. The journey below describes coaching decisions, not interface surfaces.

### Coach opens athlete

The coach arrives ready to understand someone, not ready to navigate a filing system.

They are asking:

> Who is this athlete right now, and what deserves my attention?

### Reviews Current State

The coach establishes truth before prescribing change.

They are thinking about developmental position, confidence, execution quality, consistency, and pressure response. They interpret the athlete as they are — not as a wish list, and not as a raw pile of metrics.

Decision:

> What is true about this athlete today?

### Defines Future State

With current reality clear, the coach names the destination worth coaching toward.

This is not a temporary task. It is longitudinal direction — the developmental identity the coach believes the athlete should grow into.

Decision:

> Where should this athlete be going?

### Records What Matters Next

The coach converts destination into standing guidance.

What Matters Next persists until the coach intentionally changes it. It becomes the athlete's durable coaching compass between sessions and cycles — more stable than a weekly task, more actionable than a distant ideal.

Decision:

> What must remain front of mind until I decide otherwise?

### Creates Weekly Focus

The coach turns long-term direction into this week's reinforcement.

Weekly Focus answers what this week is for and why it matters now. It is operational. It supports the developmental direction; it does not replace it.

Decision:

> What should this week reinforce, and why now?

### Reviews Behavior Under Pressure

The coach observes whether coaching is transferring when stakes rise.

Pressure reveals a different truth than casual training. The coach interprets how the athlete shows up under stress — emotionally, technically, strategically — and treats that observation as a first-class coaching signal.

Decision:

> What actually appeared when it mattered?

### Carries Forward observations

The coach closes the loop.

Carry Forward names what should persist into the next cycle so insight does not evaporate at the end of a week or competition. Continuity becomes intentional rather than accidental.

Decision:

> What must not be lost before we begin again?

Across this journey, the coach remains the author of meaning. MatMind holds the sequence, preserves context, and makes the next developmental decision easier to reach.

# Included Capabilities

Release 1 delivers the core athlete development workflow as one coherent experience.

## Current State Assessment

Establish a clear, interpretive picture of the athlete as they are now.

This includes developmental position, confidence, execution quality, consistency, and pressure response. Assessment is interpretive coaching judgment. It creates the truth from which all later direction begins.

## Future State

Define the developmental destination worth coaching toward.

Future State should feel longitudinal and intentional. It orients coaching across cycles. It is not a temporary checklist and not a substitute for weekly action.

## What Matters Next

Capture standing developmental guidance that persists until the coach intentionally changes it.

What Matters Next becomes the athlete's durable coaching compass. It bridges long-term destination and short-term reinforcement without being overwritten by the noise of a single week.

## Weekly Focus

Turn long-term direction into an active reinforcement cycle.

The coach can express what this week is for and why it matters now. Weekly Focus is the operational expression of development — practice with intention.

## Behavior Under Pressure

Observe and interpret how the athlete shows up when stakes rise.

Pressure truth becomes a first-class coaching signal. Release 1 treats under-pressure behavior as essential evidence of whether coaching is transferring into real athlete identity.

## Carry Forward

Close the loop by naming what should persist into the next cycle.

Carry Forward protects continuity. It ensures that meaningful observation becomes the starting condition for the next Assess → Develop → Train → Reinforce cycle rather than disappearing into history.

Together, these capabilities allow a coach to move an athlete through Assess → Develop → Train → Reinforce as one continuous developmental workflow.

# Explicitly Out of Scope

Release 1 is intentionally incomplete relative to the full Coach Workspace vision. The following belong to later releases and must not dilute Foundations.

### Voice Capture

Spoken drafting, voice-first meaning capture, and reusable voice patterns belong to Release 2 — Voice-first Coaching.

### Coach Presence

Voice Match Breakdown, timestamped commentary, guided playback, coach replay, director's commentary, and searchable coaching moments belong to Release 3 — Coach Presence.

### Guided Playback

Athlete-facing review shaped by coaching intent is a Presence capability, not a Foundations requirement.

### AI Pattern Recognition

Longitudinal pattern recognition, suggested development areas, and compounding intelligence belong to Release 4 — Coaching Intelligence.

### Family Dashboard

Parent views, at-home reinforcement, and family participation belong to Release 5 — Family Experience.

### Competition Intelligence

Day One competition-driven developmental intelligence belongs to later Coaching Intelligence work. Release 1 may support pressure observation as coaching judgment, but it does not deliver competition intelligence as a product layer.

### Anything belonging to Releases 2–5

If a capability depends on voice amplification, preserved teaching presence, longitudinal intelligence, or family extension, it is out of scope for Release 1.

Release 1 creates the structure those later experiences amplify. It does not attempt to become them early.

# UX Principles

Release 1 UX decisions should be judged against these principles.

### Guide coaching rather than data entry

Every interaction should feel like a coaching decision. If an interaction only produces a filled field without deepening athlete understanding, it is the wrong interaction.

### One logical coaching step at a time

The coach should move through Assess → Develop → Train → Reinforce without being forced to hold the whole sequence mentally. Progress should feel sequential and calm.

### Reduce typing

Capture should be as light as possible while remaining meaningful. Structure should reduce the need for long reconstruction. Voice amplification comes later; Release 1 still protects the coach from unnecessary writing burden.

### Reduce navigation

Context should stay with the athlete. The coach should not have to hunt across disconnected places to complete one developmental loop.

### Maintain context

Current State, Future State, What Matters Next, Weekly Focus, Behavior Under Pressure, and Carry Forward should remain intelligible as one athlete story. Returning later should feel like resuming coaching, not restarting from blank memory.

### Support long-term athlete development

The experience must favor longitudinal direction over temporary noise. Weekly work serves development. Development is not reduced to weekly work.

### Preserve coach authorship

MatMind organizes and guides. The coach remains the authority on meaning, priority, and next action.

# Success Metrics

Success is measured by coaching behavior and felt understanding — not by analytics instrumentation.

### Coaches complete assessments

Current State becomes a natural starting point rather than an optional form coaches skip.

### Developmental direction is established

Coaches define Future State and What Matters Next as standing orientation for the athlete.

### Weekly Focus becomes a recurring habit

Coaches regularly translate long-term direction into this week's reinforcement.

### Behavior Under Pressure is treated as coaching truth

Pressure observation becomes part of the developmental loop, not an afterthought.

### Carry Forward is naturally used

Coaches close cycles by naming what should persist, and those insights influence the next cycle.

### Coaches understand athlete progression

A coach returning to an athlete can see developmental continuity — where the athlete was, where they are going, what this week reinforces, and what must carry forward.

### The workspace feels like athlete understanding

The dominant feeling after use is clearer coaching judgment, not completed administration.

# Risks

These are product risks. They threaten whether Release 1 delivers coaching value even if the experience is delivered completely.

### Too many fields

If Foundations asks for more than coaches can meaningfully hold, the workflow becomes burden. Completeness can destroy adoption.

### Poor information hierarchy

If Current State, Future State, What Matters Next, Weekly Focus, Behavior Under Pressure, and Carry Forward appear equally loud, coaches will not know what matters now. Hierarchy is part of the product.

### Feels administrative instead of helpful

If the experience reads as record-keeping rather than guided development, coaches will disengage even if every capability is present.

### Workflow does not match real coaching

If the sequence forces coaches into unnatural order, language, or granularity, they will route around the product and return to memory and notes.

### Weekly work overpowers long-term direction

If Weekly Focus becomes the center of gravity, Future State and What Matters Next lose durability. Release 1 must keep operational reinforcement subordinate to developmental direction.

### Continuity fails after capture

If Carry Forward is captured but not felt in the next cycle, the loop is incomplete. Continuity is the point of closing the loop.

### Foundations try to become later releases early

Pulling voice, presence, intelligence, or family into Release 1 risks delaying the coherent developmental workflow those releases depend on.

# Future Expansion

Release 1 is designed as the structural prerequisite for the rest of the Coach Workspace roadmap.

## Release 2 — Voice-first Coaching

Foundations create the places where spoken coaching meaning belongs.

Once Current State, Future State, What Matters Next, Weekly Focus, Behavior Under Pressure, and Carry Forward exist as one workflow, voice can amplify capture without accelerating disconnected notes. Release 2 makes thinking faster. Release 1 makes thinking coherent.

## Release 3 — Coach Presence

Presence preserves the coach as teacher beyond the academy.

That teaching needs durable developmental context: what the athlete is working toward, what matters now, what showed up under pressure, and what should carry forward. Release 1 creates that authored context so later presence can attach teaching to a real developmental story.

## Release 4 — Coaching Intelligence

Intelligence compounds only when coaching authorship accumulates.

Pattern recognition, development timelines, behavior trends, and growth tracking require coherent evidence across assessment, direction, weekly reinforcement, pressure observation, and carry-forward meaning. Release 1 is the beginning of that evidence base. Without it, intelligence would summarize fragments.

## Release 5 — Family Experience

Families should reinforce coach-authored developmental truth, not invent a parallel system.

Release 1 establishes the coach-owned direction — especially What Matters Next, Weekly Focus, and Carry Forward — that the home can eventually support with clarity and confidence.

In short:

```text
Release 1 creates structure
↓
Release 2 makes capture natural
↓
Release 3 preserves teaching beyond the mat
↓
Release 4 compounds meaning over time
↓
Release 5 extends the coaching relationship into the home
```
